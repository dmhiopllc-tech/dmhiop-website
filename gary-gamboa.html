Meet Gary Gamboa

A Life Transformed by Recovery

For Gary Gamboa, recovery is not an abstract concept—it's a lived reality that shapes every decision he makes as Co-Founder and Program Director of Desert Mountain Healing IOP. In recovery since 2015, Gary's personal journey through addiction and into sustained wellness gives him unique insight into the struggles, fears, and triumphs that clients experience daily.

Gary doesn't lead from a place of theory or textbook knowledge. He leads from experience—the hard-won wisdom of someone who has walked the same path his clients are walking. He knows what it's like to feel trapped by addiction, to doubt whether recovery is possible, and to discover that with the right support, transformation is not only possible—it's sustainable.

Clients and staff describe Gary as authentic, grounded, and deeply committed to creating a treatment environment where people are treated with dignity and respect. His leadership style is rooted in compassion, accountability, and an unwavering belief that every person has the capacity to change.

Professional Background

From Personal Recovery to Program Leadership

Gary's journey into addiction treatment leadership began with his own recovery in 2015. After experiencing the transformative power of getting sober, he felt called to dedicate his life to helping others find the same freedom and hope that changed everything for him.

Over the years, Gary worked in various roles within the recovery field, gaining hands-on experience in program coordination, client support, and treatment delivery. He learned what works—and what doesn't—when it comes to helping people achieve lasting sobriety. He saw programs that treated clients with compassion and those that didn't. He experienced systems that empowered people and those that diminished them.

In 2023, Gary partnered with Sean Roberts to co-found Desert Mountain Healing IOP, bringing together their combined experience in recovery and treatment to create a different kind of facility: one built on lived experience, clinical excellence, and genuine care for every person who walks through the door.

Gary's Key Responsibilities

  • Designing and refining treatment programming to meet client needs
  • Overseeing daily program operations and ensuring quality care delivery
  • Supporting and mentoring clinical staff in best practices
  • Building a culture of accountability, compassion, and hope
  • Collaborating with the clinical director to integrate evidence-based therapies
  • Serving as a visible role model for clients in active treatment
  • Coordinating community outreach and partnership development

Gary's leadership is hands-on and relational. He's not a distant administrator—he's actively engaged with clients, staff, and the day-to-day life of the program. His presence creates a culture where recovery is not just talked about—it's lived.

Leadership Philosophy

Leading with Lived Experience and Compassion

Gary's leadership philosophy is rooted in a simple but powerful truth: The best treatment comes from people who have been there.

He believes that lived experience is not a liability—it's an asset. It's what allows him to meet clients where they are, to understand their struggles without judgment, and to offer hope grounded in real-world evidence that recovery works.

Core Leadership Values

  • Hope: Believing in the possibility of change, even when clients don't yet believe in themselves
  • Compassion: Meeting people with kindness and understanding, not judgment or shame
  • Accountability: Holding clients and staff to high standards while offering support and grace
  • Authenticity: Leading by example and being honest about the realities of recovery
  • Empowerment: Helping clients recognize their strengths and take ownership of their recovery

Gary shows up every day with the same commitment to growth, honesty, and service that he asks of his clients and staff. His leadership creates an environment where recovery isn't just possible—it's expected. And when clients see his example, they begin to believe that they, too, can build a life worth living.

Why Gary Built Desert Mountain Healing

Creating the Program He Wished He'd Had

When Gary reflects on his own recovery journey, he remembers both the moments that helped and the gaps he wished had been filled. He remembers feeling isolated, scared, and uncertain about whether he could really stay sober. He also remembers the people who believed in him, the programs that met him with compassion, and the moments when he realized that recovery was possible.

Desert Mountain Healing IOP was built to be the kind of program Gary wished he'd had access to when he was early in recovery. He wanted to create a place where:

  • Clients are treated as whole people, not just diagnoses
  • Staff in recovery are celebrated for their lived experience
  • Treatment is flexible and individualized, not one-size-fits-all
  • Accountability and compassion work hand-in-hand
  • Long-term success is prioritized over short-term metrics
  • Families are supported throughout the recovery process

Today, Desert Mountain Healing IOP reflects Gary's vision: a treatment program where people don't just stop using—they learn to build lives worth living.

What You Can Expect

Working with Gary

When you work with Gary, whether directly or through the programs he oversees, you can expect:

  • Authenticity: Gary is honest about the challenges of recovery and doesn't sugarcoat the work required
  • Respect: He treats every person with dignity, regardless of their past or their struggles
  • Accessibility: Gary maintains an open-door policy and is present in the life of the program
  • Encouragement: He celebrates progress and reminds clients of their strengths, especially on hard days
  • Lived Experience: He speaks from personal knowledge and understands the recovery journey intimately
  • Accountability: He holds people to high standards while offering grace and support along the way

Gary's leadership creates a culture where recovery is not just talked about—it's lived. And when clients see his example, they begin to believe that lasting change is possible for them, too.

Why Gary Chose This Field

Turning Pain into Purpose

Gary didn't set out to work in addiction treatment—his path into this field was born from his own experience with recovery. After getting sober in 2015, he realized that his story—his struggles, his setbacks, and his eventual transformation—could help others who were still suffering.

"When I got sober, I didn't just want to survive—I wanted to live," Gary says. "And once I started experiencing real recovery, I knew I had to give back. If I could help even one person find the hope I found, then everything I went through would be worth it."

Gary chose this field because he believes in second chances. He knows that recovery isn't linear, and he's committed to walking alongside clients through every setback and every victory. His nine years of sobriety stand as proof that lasting change is possible—and every day, he shows up to help others discover that truth for themselves.
